One Common Language

Three rulebooks, one set of duties, compared line by line

There is a routine in mathematics named after D. R. Kaprekar. Take any four-digit number with at least two different digits. Sort its digits from high to low and from low to high, keeping any leading zeros, then subtract the smaller from the larger, and repeat.

You always arrive at 6174. Any starting point, one fixed procedure, the same verified answer.

That is how compliance should work. Two organizations with the same profile should arrive at the same required program, by the same procedure, with every step traceable to its source.

That idea is why I started CLHEAR, an open standard for describing compliance programs. It begins with the exact wording of each law and regulation. Clauses that say the same thing in different rulebooks become one duty, linked back to every source that states it. Each duty is then broken down into what a company actually needs in place: people, documents, systems, and processes.

Describe a company, with its licenses, products, and customers, and the standard shows the program that company needs.

Here is what that looks like with real rules.

The UK wording follows the EU wording almost word for word. FINRA’s wording differs, but the duties overlap. CLHEAR records each duty once and links it to every clause that asserts it.

The standard is designed to keep running. When a regulation comes into force, new duties appear. When one expires, the duties it created are retired.

Anyone should be able to describe an organization and see what compliance requires of it. A standard earns trust only when people outside the company that wrote it can test it, challenge it, and improve it. That is why CLHEAR is developed in the open, at github.com/Reg42-ai/clhear.
