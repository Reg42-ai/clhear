The Wall

Why hiring more people no longer keeps up with the rules

Finance runs on a general ledger. Sales runs on a CRM. To my knowledge, compliance is the one critical function at large financial institutions that still has no leading single source of truth.

Without one, a program lives in pieces. Policies, roles, committees, systems, and evidence each sit in their own place, in their own vocabulary. The connections between them have to be held by people.

I have owned that kind of stack. I came to believe the risk sits less in any one application than in the spaces between them.

I also believe adding a jurisdiction should not make a compliance program much more complex than covering one. Without a common language, it does.

Now add AI. Firms are putting models into marketing, client communications, trading, and operations. Those models bring obligations of their own, and accountability for what they do stays with the firm.

For a firm that operates nationwide, each of those state laws is another text to check for applicability. Across the G20, national AI laws arrive at a steadier pace: between 11 and 16 a year since 2020.

That is the wall. More rules, more applications, more AI, and no shared map. I don’t believe more headcount gets a firm over it. A common language can.
